# III/2. Open questions

1. **The sharp test of the complex weights.** The [4th law](../I-language/I-05-contract_en.md) states why they are needed (the engine turns phase as well); but an ordinary interference experiment can be recomputed with real weights too (in a doubled state space), so it does not separate them. The sharp test is the network Bell experiment ([Renou et al., 2021; experimental confirmation 2022](../appendix/C-benchmarks_en.md)), which provably cannot be reproduced with real weights — recomputing this is the goal.

2. **The origin of locality — selection, after II/12.** The first half of the question is closed ([II/11](../II-proofs/II-11-locality-readout/proof_en.md)): adjacency is a view of the finished state, readable from the inside, and its signature is the surface law. For the second half the candidate — "connectedness is enforced by exclusion" — failed in its naive form ([II/12](../II-proofs/II-12-network-race/proof_en.md)): with a free network both vetted contracts collapse (the bond into a pairwise covering, smoothness into a single fat contract and vacuum parking — the collapse theorem). The rescue route is the equal rank of the sites ([III/1, 5.](III-01-candidate-laws_en.md)): with it, density yields the connected ring from five instances onwards, but the sharp law is missing (the half-filling and degree-resonant exceptions — the candidate for handling them is the shell-fit rule, [III/1, 6.](III-01-candidate-laws_en.md)). The root signal: in the language the contract is a free resource — in physics the coupling is carried by the instances; selection may be decided where the contracts themselves are born (question 7).

3. **Which liquid?** On the Kagome lattice the liquid-versus-frozen decision is a theorem ([II/9](../II-proofs/II-09-kagome/proof_en.md)); whether the winning liquid is gapped or gapless is open — the liquid beats the best structured solid candidate by only 0.003 per bond, and deciding this requires systems of a hundred objects. Here it would no longer be a matter of recomputing but of stating something first — and the concept of question 7 may be needed to decide it.

4. **Why is the electron excluding?** The class assignment is a declaration in the language; physics derives it from relativity (the spin–statistics theorem). The question is whether there is a route within the language. A separate branch is the first computed proof of the sharing class (the common lowest state of many sharing instances — the Bose–Einstein condensate of physics) and the 2, 8, 20 filling of the three-extension bowl (the first magic numbers of atomic nuclei).

5. **Many-triangle lattices, further on.** The terrain after [II/9](../II-proofs/II-09-kagome/proof_en.md): larger frustrated networks, where physics too has no closed solution — the long-term terrain of the language.

6. **The infinite form of the internal witness.** A sharp phase or a dramatic slowdown? In the language the decisive question is a single sentence — the **avalanche**: a rare inclusion of locally weak unevenness may become an internal witness core and slowly witness the whole; everything depends on whether the witnessing power of the core grows faster than its access weakens with distance. A separate branch is the within-language derivation of the weak half of the theorem (that witnessing spreads).

7. **The object born from a pattern.** The objects of the language are elementary; it has no concept for the case where the joint pattern of many elementary objects itself begins to behave as an independent object. Yet the reality check of [II/9](../II-proofs/II-09-kagome/proof_en.md) already refers to such a thing (fractionalized excitations), and physics too states the "which liquid" decision (3.) in terms of the kind of the excitations. The missing concept: the derived type — and for it the assumption of the two-class theorem (that two exchanges amount to nothing) is not obligatory, so here the language may open new type classes. (Physics calls the object born from a pattern a quasiparticle, and the types outside the two classes anyons.) The root signal of II/12 points here as well: if the coupling is carried by the instances themselves (by the patterns of their fields), then the solution of the selection question (2.) may be born here too.

8. **The extension number — after II/16.** The coordination scan has run ([II/16](../II-proofs/II-16-coordination-ten/proof_en.md)): at coordination ten the staircase skips three (line, plane, four extensions, five extensions), the top is taken by the only main candidate carrying the mirror mark — the native five — and the top of the full field by the bipartite line member. The unified law of the three proofs ([III/1, 8.](III-01-candidate-laws_en.md)): **the top is taken by the lowest-extension member of the field carrying the mirror mark** — its theorem half is the hole-mirror and the direction theorem, its measured half is threefold, without exception. "Why three" has thereby definitively changed shape: at coordination six and eight the lowest mirror carrier *happens* to be three. Its open questions: *the mirror-carrier map* (derivable, even outside a proof): at which coordinations which rung is the lowest weave carrying the mirror mark; *the carrier question:* why the mirror chooses — turning [III/1, 7.](III-01-candidate-laws_en.md) into a theorem; and *the final form of the density question, handed to the root program (question 7):* **why is the contract structure of the world such that its lowest mirror carrier is three?** Side-open questions: the reason for the omission of three at ten; the ~half pattern of the dominance gap. An unchanged honest finding: the 1/r attraction import is untouched — the chain now stands as follows: the form of attraction gives three from outside; in the race the mirror and the density give it from inside; the meeting of the two is the business of the root program.
