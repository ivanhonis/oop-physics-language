# I/1. The idea and the yardstick

The idea in one sentence: the physical world can be described with object-oriented patterns, provided that every pattern is sharpened into a computable claim and checked against a measured result.

**The methodological rule.** Every new pattern remains a metaphor until it produces a number. The procedure for extending the language is fixed: (1) stating the pattern in OOP language, (2) writing down the mathematical form, (3) recomputing a known experimental or published computed result. Whatever does not pass (3) does not enter the language.

**The import ledger.** The rule above measures whether the language tells the truth; it does not measure whether it helps to think. The yardstick for that: every derivation must be carried out strictly within the concepts of the language, and every concept borrowed from outside must be recorded. Three outcomes: *it helps* — the key idea is stated first by the structure of the language, and the mathematics only confirms it; *it is leaky* — few, well-delimited imports are needed, and these are the next concepts of the language to be extended; *it does not help* — there are so many imports that the path is in fact the conventional physical derivation, relabeled. Every chapter of Part II closes with an import ledger; the total import of the language so far is three items (locality — since [II/11](../II-proofs/II-11-locality-readout/proof_en.md) a theorem by half, its remainder being the [open question of selection](../III-frontier/III-02-open-questions_en.md) —, the [form of attraction](../II-proofs/II-04-hydrogen-atom/proof_en.md), [two type declarations for the electron](../II-proofs/II-07-bowl-magic-numbers/proof_en.md)); everything else was born inside.

**The package method.** From II/13 onwards the larger derivations are produced with a rulebook fixed in advance, in individually checked derivation packages (question — inputs — derivation — verdict — outgoing claims), under a gate rule: the next package may build on a package only after its approval, and a chapter is assembled exclusively from the outgoing claims of accepted packages. Fixing the rulebook before the computation excludes any after-the-fact modification of the rules — this is what keeps the prediction refutable. The trail: [Appendix D](../appendix/D-package-method_en.md).
