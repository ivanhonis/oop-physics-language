# III/1. Candidate laws and theorems

1. **The law of "being a single object" (candidate).** The smoothness contract is what binds many sites into a single object — not an import but the definitional price of the concept "one object". Half of the theorem is confirmed ([II/6](../II-proofs/II-06-universality/proof_en.md)): the form of the mixing contract does not matter, the bottom of every local mixing is the same. What is missing: the derivation of locality itself ([2nd open question](III-02-open-questions_en.md)).

2. **The derivability of the bond contract (candidate).** The mechanism of [II/8](../II-proofs/II-08-exclusion-vs-repulsion/proof_en.md), with the opposite sign in two neighboring wells, yields the role-forbidding contract of [II/1](../II-proofs/II-01-pair-bond/proof_en.md). If the derivation holds, the first vetted contract of the language turns from an axiom into a theorem, and the strength of the coupling from a supplied number into a derived one. Target proof: the bond strength of the hydrogen molecule.

3. **The internal-witness theorem (confirmed on a small piece).** [II/10](../II-proofs/II-10-internal-witness/proof_en.md) computed both halves; the infinite form is open ([6th open question](III-02-open-questions_en.md)).

4. **The output is not data (candidate theorem).** The measurement output is not a field of the system but a scope-bound view fact — a corollary of the [2nd law](../I-language/I-04-ownership-view_en.md), with zero import. The nested witness (the laboratory is to the outside observer what the particle is to the one inside) is the recursion of witnessing ([I/7](../I-language/I-07-measurement_en.md)): inside, a branch-wise view with an output; outside, a single, purely rotating joint state, as long as the encapsulation is perfect — a single leaking record bit redraws the ownership boundary, and then it becomes witnessed outside as well. The two descriptions do not conflict, because "which branch was realized" is not a field of the system; the in-principle reversal by the outside observer does not rewrite a fact but erases the view to which the fact belonged. Target proof: recomputing the published violation values of the nested-observer experiments (2019–2020). (Physics: Wigner's friend; the no-go theorem is [Frauchiger–Renner, 2018](../appendix/C-benchmarks_en.md).)

5. **The equal rank of the sites (candidate).** There is no distinguished site: the contract network looks the same from every site. In [II/12](../II-proofs/II-12-network-race/proof_en.md) this is what closes the vacuum parking — without it the collapse is proved, with it density enforces connectedness. Its status is twofold: as a statement it is a new import; as a direction it is the extension of the [5th law](../I-language/I-06-identity_en.md) to the sites (the renaming test of [II/11](../II-proofs/II-11-locality-readout/proof_en.md) already pointed this way). What is missing: its derivation, and the sharp selection law — understanding the degree-resonant exceptions.

6. **The shell-fit rule (candidate, its scope refined in II/14).** The network race is decided by degree filling: the winning weave is the one in whose ladder a degree is just filling up at the given filling. Alongside it stand the degree resonances of II/12, the complete winner table of [II/13](../II-proofs/II-13-dimension-race/proof_en.md) (the plane wins at its closed degree of 11) and the lower comb of [II/14](../II-proofs/II-14-dimension-staircase/proof_en.md) (the line alternates with its own disconnected multiples in a residue-class pattern). **Against it** stands the decisive boundary of II/14: the plane–space switch is decided not by degree filling but by the mirror (candidate 7) — space takes over the race in the *middle* of its central shelf, exactly at half filling. What is missing: a sharp statement of the scope of the two mechanisms — when the degree decides, and when the mirror. [II/15](../II-proofs/II-15-dimension-fourth-rung/proof_en.md) narrows it further: the upper band boundaries are not shelf boundaries (they fall 2, 3 and 112 fillings from the nearest closed degree) — the band boundary is the intersection point of the cost curves. The picture in [II/16](../II-proofs/II-16-coordination-ten/proof_en.md) is mixed: two band boundaries are sharp to 1 and 3 fillings from the incoming closed degree, that of the native at 5338 is not — the question of scope remains open.

7. **The mirror rule (candidate — new, from II/14).** The upper hemisphere of the filling is won by the network whose ladder is symmetric about the degree average and is the widest (the bipartite mark: the network has no odd cycle, so the ladder is mirror-symmetric) — the mirror pushes the unfilled beats highest. Evidence: the space band of [II/14](../II-proofs/II-14-dimension-staircase/proof_en.md) opens exactly at half filling, and the full end is taken by all-odd-step lines (themselves carrying the bipartite mark). It has two edges: the mark is **not** a property of extension (it can be produced on a line too — which is why it is not enough for selection on its own), and the same mirror spoils the readout at small size (the antipodal echo — the readout limit of II/14). What is missing: a sharp statement and proof, and its relation to the shell rule (6.). [II/15](../II-proofs/II-15-dimension-fourth-rung/proof_en.md) sharpened it and also brought a surprise: the upper half is won **exactly** by the pair carrying the mirror mark — every upper filling is theirs — but within the pair the order reverses at the top (candidate 8). [II/16](../II-proofs/II-16-coordination-ten/proof_en.md) is the third, exceptionless confirmation: at coordination ten the upper half belongs to the only main candidate carrying the mirror mark (the native five), and the top of the full field to the bipartite line member.

8. **The top law — unified form (with two theorem legs; the carrier half measured).** The top is taken by the lowest-extension member of the field carrying the mirror mark. Its three components: *(i)* the top is ruled by those carrying the mirror mark — measured, across three coordinations (six, eight, ten) without exception (candidate 7); *(ii)* among those carrying the mirror mark the full end is the mirror of the sparse end — the [hole-mirror theorem](../II-proofs/II-15-dimension-fourth-rung/PKG-15-5-hole-mirror_en.md), confirmed at ten in [PKG-16-5](../II-proofs/II-16-coordination-ten/PKG-16-5-transfer_en.md); *(iii)* when sparse the lower extension is the cheaper one — the [direction theorem](../II-proofs/II-15-dimension-fourth-rung/PKG-15-6-direction_en.md), with certified stretches at both large coordinations. The evidence series: at six the top belongs to space (the only bipartite main candidate), at eight to space (the lower one of the bipartite pair), at ten to the five-extension native (the only bipartite main candidate), and in the full field to the bipartite line member — the earlier "top-three" reading was a shadow of this. What is missing: the derivation of the carrier half (why the mirror chooses — turning candidate 7 into a theorem) and the mirror-carrier map (at which coordination which rung is the lowest bipartite weave).
