# II/4. The hydrogen atom

**The question.** The same construction on a sharp, measured target: does the contract yield the spectrum of hydrogen?

**The system and the contract.** In place of the wall, an **attractive** term: sites close to the center receive a cost discount, in inverse proportion to the distance.

**The computation and the result.** Since the contract measures only distance and not direction, the simplest of the self-rotating patterns are themselves directionless. Their cost ladder has the ratio **1 : 1/4 : 1/9 : 1/16** — computed: −0.5; −0.125; −0.0556; −0.0313 in atomic units, agreeing to four decimal places with the measured spectrum; with the physical coupling strength the first rung is the measured 13.6 eV. Two unrequested bonuses: the lowest rung of the **direction-dependent** patterns falls exactly on the second rung of the ladder — this is the measured level coincidence of hydrogen, which nobody prescribed for the contract — and above zero cost the ladder disappears, the levels merge: this is the measured ionization. The complete claim — quantized when bound, not when free — appeared within a single system, computed.

**Checking against reality.** The measured spectrum, level coincidence and ionization threshold of hydrogen ([Appendix C](../../appendix/C-benchmarks_en.md)).

**Import ledger:** one item — the **form of attraction** (distance-inverse discount). **What it confirmed:** the quantization theorem with sharp numbers; the unrequested bonuses of level coincidence and ionization.
