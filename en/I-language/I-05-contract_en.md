# I/5. The contract: judge and engine

**The contract as loss function.** Every relation places an expectation on the joint state of the two objects: it says how large a **penalty** each joint state receives. The total cost of the system is the sum of the penalties. The equilibrium state is the one that minimizes the total cost. (Physics calls the total cost energy, and the minimum the ground state.) "The system does not fall apart" = the cost of the joint state is smaller than that of the separated parts; the difference is the **binding energy** — the yield of joint ownership.

**The system does not strive — the system+environment ensemble slides.** In a closed system the engine is cost-preserving (this is the conservation of energy): a high-cost state circulates by itself at high cost forever. Only a system that can hand its surplus over to an **environment** is able to slide downwards. The analogy with the training of neural networks is literal here: training is the physical process in which the system scatters the penalty into its environment. (In practice physics searches for ground states in exactly this way, lately with real neural networks too — "neural quantum states".)

**The environment pushes back as well.** The balance of handover and push-back is the temperature: the system does not settle into the pure minimum but into a weighting across its levels set by the temperature — the pure ground state is enforced only by a cold environment. The settled state is not a pure state but a view: the environment as witness mixes it out. (Physics calls this a thermal state.)

**Fourth law: the contract is not only judge but also engine.** The very same total-cost function that scores the states also determines the evolution of the system: at every instant it says in which direction and how fast the weights rotate. The system has no separate behavior beyond its contracts — the contract system itself is the program that runs. (Physics: energy generates the time evolution; the name of the equation is the Schrödinger equation. Establishing proof: [II/2](../II-proofs/II-02-exchange-oscillation/proof_en.md).)

Three consequences:

1. **The ground state is the one the engine rotates into itself.** Equilibrium is not a stopping but a self-rotating pattern. That is why it is stable.
2. **In a bound system the engine has discrete beats.** The rotation generated by the contract has only definite frequencies — the tones of the system. The discrete value set is not written into the type but comes out of the beats of the engine (proof: [II/3](../II-proofs/II-03-box/proof_en.md), [II/4](../II-proofs/II-04-hydrogen-atom/proof_en.md)).
3. **This is where the complex weight becomes a constraint.** The engine turns not only the magnitude of the weights but also their phase — with real weights this rotation generally cannot be described. The complex numbers are the price of the fourth law. (Its sharp experimental test: [Part III, 1st open question](../III-frontier/III-02-open-questions_en.md).)
